## 1. "Why the Fed raising one number changes your mortgage"
**News hook:** On Sept 16, 2026 the Fed raised the federal funds target range to 3.75-4.00%, its first hike since 2023 (CNBC headline via search only: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html, fetch returned 403). Primary, opened: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm (range "3-3/4 to 4 percent"; interest on reserve balances 3.90%; discount rate 4.0%; effective Sept 17).
**Known thing:** mortgage, savings account, credit card.
**Mechanism:**
1. Banks hold reserve accounts at the Fed. The Fed pays them interest on those reserves (3.90% now; opened release).
2. A bank will not lend reserves to another bank for less than it can earn risk-free at the Fed, so the Fed's rate sets a floor under overnight lending.
3. The Fed also offers overnight reverse repo at 3.75% (opened release), a second floor for non-bank money funds. The market overnight rate lands inside the 3.75-4.00% range.
4. Banks price their own loans and deposits off that overnight cost of money (UNVERIFIED-STEP: needs a Fed/BIS explainer).
5. Longer rates (mortgages) also depend on expected future Fed moves and inflation, so they move by anticipation, not one-for-one (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
6. Higher borrowing cost reduces spending and borrowing, which cools price pressure (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
**Misconceptions:** (a) the Fed prints a rate or dictates mortgage rates directly; (b) the Fed "sets" the overnight rate by decree rather than by paying interest on reserves; (c) a hike makes all rates rise immediately by 0.25.
**Facts:** target range 3.75-4.00%, IORB 3.90%, discount 4.0%, effective Sept 17, standing repo 4.0%, reverse repo 3.75% (all opened Fed release above). Vote 12-0 and Middle East tension as a factor: search-result only, UNVERIFIED.
**Define:** federal funds rate, reserves, basis point, repo.
**Animation:** bank "tubes" with a floor line (reverse repo) and ceiling (discount), the market rate bouncing between; ripple arrows to mortgage and savings.
**Difficulty:** 3. **Time-sensitivity:** evergreen mechanism, news peg decays within weeks (next FOMC date not verified). **Demand guess:** high (guess). **Audiences:** K1 A5 O4 T4.

## 2. "Why AI data centers are breaking the power grid auction"
**News hook:** Sept 29, 2026 FERC rejected parts of PJM's plan for a one-time backstop capacity auction; PJM said Sept 30 it would not open on schedule. Opened: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-delays-backstop-procurement-ferc-data-center/831751/ (target 6.8 GW, cap $555/MW-day, delivery year 2028/29). Also UN/UNECE, Sept 8, opened: https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168296. Bloomberg (search only): https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/biggest-us-grid-suspends-plan-for-new-data-center-power-auction.
**Known thing:** the electricity bill, AI chatbots.
**Mechanism:**
1. Grid supply must equal demand every second (UNVERIFIED-STEP: cite NERC/EIA).
2. A grid operator forecasts peak demand years ahead and must ensure enough generators are committed: the "capacity auction" pays generators to be available.
3. Data centers add large, fast-growing, sometimes spiky loads; the UN piece says they can be built in 2-5 years while grid expansion takes 10+ (opened).
4. If forecast demand exceeds offered supply, the auction clears at the price cap, with no new supply attracted (PJM July result: search-result only, https://www.sierraclub.org/press-releases/2026/07/pjm-capacity-auction-hits-price-cap-third-consecutive-time).
5. Regulators then argue over who pays for backup capacity: FERC wants costs tied to updated load forecasts, i.e., data centers (opened Utility Dive).
6. Result: delay, higher capacity prices that flow into customer bills (last link UNVERIFIED-STEP).
**Misconceptions:** AI "uses most electricity" (UN: 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh by 2030, opened, is data centers overall); power plants can be built in months; the auction sells electricity itself rather than availability.
**Facts:** above plus EIA US generation 4.43 trillion kWh in 2025, gas about 41% (opened https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/electricity-in-the-us.php).
**Define:** MW vs GW, capacity vs energy, price cap, grid operator.
**Animation:** a seesaw of supply vs demand over a timeline where the "data center" bar grows faster than the "new plants" bar.
**Difficulty:** 4. **Time-sensitivity:** news-driven, mechanism evergreen. **Demand:** medium-high (guess). **Audiences:** K1 A4 O3 T5.

**Mechanism:**

3. A lower orbit is faster, so the capsule gains on the station; thruster burns raise its orbit in steps (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
4. Closer in, it stops at hold points and the station/crew verify safety (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
5. The capsule flies in autonomously and docks to Harmony's forward port (opened NASA release).
6. Why faster this time: UNVERIFIED, the reason (fewer orbits/phasing plan) was not in any opened page.
**Misconceptions:** you aim at where the ISS is; orbiting means no gravity; speeding up moves you to a higher orbit directly (it first raises the far side, then you slow relative to the ground).
**Facts:** 11:10 a.m. EDT launch, ~7 p.m. docking, Harmony forward, crew of four incl. Watkins, Delaney, Kutryk, Teteryatnikov (all opened NASA).
**Define:** orbit, phasing, rendezvous, docking port.

## 4. "Sodium batteries: the cheap cousin of lithium"
**News hook:** Sept 22, 2026 South Korea roadmap: sodium-ion 160 Wh/kg by 2027, 220 Wh/kg by 2030 (pv magazine, Sept 30, opened: http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/30/south-korea-marks-major-shift-to-sodium-ion-batteries-to-regain-market/). China's 15th five-year battery plan, Sept 28, targets large-scale all-solid-state use by 2030 (search only: https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/28/china-unveils-5-year-battery-plan).
**Known thing:** phone/EV battery.
**Mechanism:**
1. Battery has two electrodes with an electrolyte between them.
2. Charging pushes ions (Na+ here, Li+ in lithium) into the negative electrode while electrons go round the outside charger circuit.
3. Discharging: ions drift back, electrons flow through your device and do work.
4. Sodium ions are heavier and bigger, so the cell stores less energy per kilo (the 160-220 Wh/kg targets, opened, versus solid-state 400 Wh/kg target, opened).
5. Sodium is abundant (UNVERIFIED-STEP: cite USGS), so cost can fall, but opened article says it is currently more expensive.
**Misconceptions:** batteries store electrons; sodium batteries are already cheaper; solid-state means no lithium.
**Facts:** LFP about 90% of storage market (opened pv magazine); $8 bn company investment 2026-2030; $300 m research 2027-2031 (opened).
**Define:** ion, electrolyte, Wh/kg.
**Animation:** ions shuttling between two slabs while electrons take the outside loop.
**Difficulty:** 3. **Time-sensitivity:** evergreen. **Demand:** medium-high (guess). **Audiences:** K3 A5 O3 T4.

## 6. "Why a fusion reactor needs to be colder than space"
**News hook:** CFS reported its cryogenic plant ran the full system at 8 K (Sept 16, 2026) per Fusion Fortnightly Sept 29 (opened: https://www.fusionconclusion.com/Fusion-Forthnightly-2026-09-29/). Primary CFS source not opened; "140 MW" quote in that page is unclear, UNVERIFIED.
**Known thing:** the Sun, MRI machines.
**Mechanism:**
1. Fuse hydrogen isotopes at ~100 million degrees (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
2. At that heat matter is plasma, which would melt any wall, so magnetic fields hold it.
3. Strong fields need huge currents; superconducting coils carry them with near-zero resistance only when very cold (the 8 K milestone).
4. Cold magnets next to a hot plasma means insulation (vacuum) between them (UNVERIFIED-STEP).
**Misconceptions:** it is "unlimited energy now"; the plasma touches walls; superconductors are free. **Difficulty:** 4. **Evergreen:** yes. **Demand:** medium (guess). **Audiences:** K3 A4 O3 T5. Needs primary-source work before use.
