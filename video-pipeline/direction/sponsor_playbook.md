# Sponsor Playbook

Evidence tags: [OFFICIAL] = primary regulator/platform page fetched; [SECONDARY] = blog/agency, unverified; [OUR JUDGMENT] = editorial rule aligned with regulations; [U] = unverified claim, marked explicitly.

---

## 6 Integration Formats

| # | Format | How it works | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Native example** | Sponsor product is the worked example inside the explanation (e.g., a savings app shown while explaining compound interest). Requires a truthful, real-use demonstration. | Highest retention; concepts, economics |
| 2 | **Product as prop** | Product appears in a demo or experiment scene; it is a supporting character, not the hero. | Food science, experiment formats |
| 3 | **Sponsored explainer** | Entire video covers the sponsor's domain or product category. Highest rate; label from the first beat. All editorial claims must be substantiated. | Case study, Why-is-X formats |
| 4 | **Affiliate** | Link/code in description or pinned comment; no flat fee. Material connection still requires disclosure. | Versus/comparison format |
| 5 | **Ad-read bumper** | Fixed 5–10 s segment, typically at end (preferred) or start. Easy to sell and reuse across topics. | Any format as a last beat; weakest retention impact |
| 6 | **Series sponsorship** | Brand "presents" a recurring character series for N episodes. Predictable income; fits character-series format. | Character series (format 14 in catalog) |

## Honest-Integration Rules [OUR JUDGMENT, aligned with FTC]

1. **Remove test:** take out the sponsor beat; the story must still make complete sense.
2. **Add test:** put the sponsor beat back; every claim in it must still be true.
3. **No invented capabilities.** Never say the product does something it cannot.
4. **No health or financial claims without substantiation.** Consult a qualified source; disclose limitations.
5. **Synthetic narrator rule.** A TTS voice cannot "experience" a product. Rephrase "I use this every day" as "here is what it actually does" with a real demonstration.
6. **If the product does not fit the concept, do not force it.** A bad native integration reads as an ad-read anyway, with worse retention.

## Disclosure Requirements

### FTC (US) [OFFICIAL — ftc.gov]
- Disclose any financial, employment, personal, or family relationship, including free or discounted products.
- Placement: alongside the endorsement—not buried in a "more" section, end of description, or profile. In video: inside the video itself, both audio AND visual form.
- Wording: plain language — "ad," "sponsored," "thanks to [Brand] for the free product." Avoid "sp," "spon," "collab," "ambassador."
- For AI-generated synthetic content: FTC endorsement rules apply; virtual/AI influencers are covered (secondary summaries; confirm wording at ftc.gov). [SECONDARY for AI-specific wording]
- FTC 2024 fake-reviews rule covers AI-generated testimonials. [U — background knowledge; re-verify before relying on it]

### YouTube Paid Promotion [OFFICIAL — support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235]
- Check "paid promotion" toggle in YouTube Studio; YouTube adds an in-video label at the start.
- The toggle does NOT replace FTC-style on-screen and audio disclosure inside the video.
- Content must still meet Community Guidelines and ad policies.
- AI-altered content policy (synthetic-media label): required when content could be mistaken for a real person, event, or place. Illustrated/animated content does not require the AI label. [SECONDARY — NBC news coverage of official policy]

### Instagram / Meta [SECONDARY — contentgrip.com; unverified against Meta primary docs]
- Use the branded-content tool to add "Paid partnership with [brand]" to posts, Reels, and Stories.
- Platform label alone does not satisfy FTC; add spoken disclosure in the first few seconds AND caption disclosure before the "more" truncation.
- From March 2026: brands must manually disclose third-party AI creative tools in commercial ads. [SECONDARY, unverified]
- Meta may auto-label content it detects as AI. Creators can also enable an "AI Creator" profile label. [SECONDARY]

## Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Native example (compound interest + savings app)**
Topic: "How your money grows while you sleep."
Beat placement: THEREFORE beat. After showing the cookie-town analogy for compounding, cut to a real screen recording of the savings app's interest-growth chart. Narration: "A savings account does exactly this. [Brand] shows you the curve in real time. Rates change—check the current rate in the app." On-screen text: "SPONSORED BY [BRAND]" in the first 3 s of the sponsor beat. Voice says "sponsored by [Brand]" at the start.
Remove test: analogy works without the app. Add test: the app genuinely shows compound interest.

**Example 2 — Product as prop (bread-staling + airtight container brand)**
Topic: "Why bread goes stale."
Beat placement: kitchen tip beat (last). After explaining moisture migration, the mascot places bread in the sponsor's container. Narration: "An airtight container slows this down—that's the whole mechanism working in reverse. [Brand] sent us one to test." Disclosure on screen from first frame of that scene. No claim beyond "slows moisture migration."
Remove test: the mechanism explanation is complete before the prop appears.

**Example 3 — Ad-read bumper (any topic + productivity app)**
Beat placement: after the CTA, separate from the story. Narration: "This video is sponsored by [Brand]. If you want [specific use case], [Brand] does [specific feature]. Link in bio." Duration: 7 s. On-screen: "SPONSOR" text from start of beat.
Note: ad-read bumpers generate the least friction when placed after the main story payoff rather than before.

## Pitch Outline

1. **Concept first:** "A 45s explainer on [brand's topic area], with your product as [native example / prop / sponsored explainer]."
2. **Evidence package:** 2–3 sample videos in the exact format + per-video views, average view duration, audience geography (top countries).
3. **Package offer:** e.g., 3 Shorts + 1 carousel + usage-rights window. Name the disclosure approach upfront.
4. **Pilot price:** offer a reduced pilot rate for the first integration; full rate on renewal.
5. **Channels:** direct outreach to brands in your topic pillars; Instagram Creator Marketplace; YouTube BrandConnect; creator agencies.

## Media Kit Fields

- Channel one-liner and niche
- Audience demographics: age range, top countries, gender split (from analytics)
- Per-video performance: median views (not best), average view duration (AVD), audience retention curve shape
- Subscriber / follower counts per platform
- Format menu with deliverables and turnaround time
- Past results or case study (view count + AVD before/after for a comparable topic)
- Brand-safety statement: topics avoided, AI-use disclosure policy, fact-check policy
- Rate card or "starting at" (only publish after you have real data; [U] industry benchmarks conflict widely and have no stated methodology)
- Contact and turnaround time

> Rate benchmarks from blogs are unreliable and conflict. [SECONDARY, U] Build your own rate card from actual brand conversations once you have consistent view counts.
