# Video Formats and Sponsor Integration (Shorts / Reels / Carousels)

Date: 2026-10-05. Scope: faceless, AI-assisted explainer channel. Stack: Remotion illustrations, AI clips, manim. Confidence tags: [OFFICIAL] = platform/regulator page fetched; [SECONDARY] = blog/agency, unverified; [OUR JUDGMENT] = format analysis from general craft knowledge, not sourced.

## 1. Format taxonomy (short form, 20-75s)

Cost key: Low = Remotion text/illustration only; Med = plus AI clips/stock; High = custom animation, manim, or heavy QA.

| # | Format | Best topics | Structure | Length | Cost with our stack | Retention risk | Sponsor fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ELI5 story-wrapped | concepts, economics, physics | Hook question > tiny character/situation > analogy > payoff line | 40-60s | Low-Med (Remotion illustrations) | Analogy drift; slow setup | High: brand is the "character/tool" in the story |
| 2 | "What if" | physics, science, places | Absurd premise > step-by-step consequences > surprising limit | 45-60s | Med (AI clips) | Needs real numbers or reads as fluff | Medium |
| 3 | Myth-busting | food, science, economics | State myth > "actually" > evidence > rule of thumb | 30-45s | Low | Low; strong curiosity gap | Medium-High (product tests a myth) |
| 4 | "Why is X" | all | Observation > cause chain (2-3 links) > twist | 35-55s | Low-Med | Low if hook is concrete | High |
| 5 | Versus / comparison | economics, food, places, products | Two options > 3 criteria > verdict | 40-60s | Low (split-screen templates) | Verdict must be earned | Highest for affiliate; keep editorial independence |
| 6 | History/timeline in 60s | economics, places, science | Dated beats, one visual per beat, callback ending | 55-75s | Med | Date-dump fatigue | Low-Med |
| 7 | News explainer ("what happened / why it matters") | current events | Event > 1 line context > stakes > what to watch | 30-50s | Med; speed-sensitive | Accuracy and sourcing risk; sensitive-topic limits (see 5) | Low (brand safety); series sponsor only |
| 8 | Case study / mini-doc | economics, food, places | Hook outcome > setup > turning point > lesson | 60-90s (or 2-part) | Med-High | Length vs Shorts feed | High: brand can be the case |
| 9 | Place / travel explainer | places, experiences | Map zoom > one surprising fact > how it works locally > tip | 35-55s | Med (AI clips risk misrepresenting real places; label if realistic) | Generic-AI look | High (travel, booking, gear) |
| 10 | Food science | food | Weird result > mechanism > kitchen tip | 30-50s | Low-Med | Low | High (cookware, ingredients) |
| 11 | Experiment / demonstration | physics, science, food | Predict > run > reveal > why | 30-60s | High (needs real or simulated footage) | Fake-looking sims hurt trust | High (product as apparatus) |
| 12 | Visual math proof | math | Puzzle > one animated insight > "aha" > generalise | 30-60s | High (manim; scripting effort) | Audience niche; pacing | Medium (education apps) |
| 13 | Data / chart story | economics, current events | One chart animates > annotate the change > "so what" | 30-50s | Low (Remotion charts) | Misleading scales; cite source on screen | Medium-High (finance tools; mind health/finance policy limits) |
| 14 | Character series | all (recurring mascot) | Fixed cast and format, new topic each episode | 30-60s | Med upfront, low after | Template sameness: the main inauthentic-content risk | Highest: series sponsorship |

## 3. Sponsor integration formats

| Format | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Native example | Sponsor product is the worked example inside the explanation (e.g., a budgeting app shown while explaining compound interest) | Best retention; must be a real, truthful use; disclose |
| Product as prop | Product appears in the demo/experiment (format 11, 10) | Low friction; viewers may not notice, so on-screen disclosure matters |
| Sponsored explainer | Whole video is about sponsor's domain or product category | Highest rate; label early; editorial claims must be substantiated |
| Affiliate | Link/code in description/pinned comment; no flat fee | Still a material connection: disclose |
| Ad-read bumper | 5-10s fixed segment, start or end | Easy to sell and reuse; weakest retention |
| Series sponsorship | Brand "presents" a recurring character series for N episodes | Predictable income; fits format 14 |

### What brands pay small channels (all figures [SECONDARY], unverified, vendor blogs with no stated methodology)
- Micro-creators (10K-100K subs): integrations about $200-$1,500; dedicated long-form about $1,000-$5,000; Shorts about $100-$1,000 (https://seowebster.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates-pricing-guide-micro-influencers and the search summary of related pages).
- Another source claims dedicated Shorts at $300-$2,000 for 10K-100K subs, Shorts CPM roughly $12-$22, and Shorts priced at 20-40% of long-form rates (https://influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-shorts-creator-rates/).
- General YouTube sponsorship CPM claimed at $15-$80 depending on niche (https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/).
- The ranges disagree and no source here states how it was gathered. Treat as pitch-anchoring only; do not forecast revenue from them. Brands rarely pay at all below a few thousand consistent views per video [OUR JUDGMENT]; start with affiliate or product-for-content.

### How small creators pitch
1. Pitch a concept, not a slot: "a 45s explainer on [brand's category problem], with your product as the worked example."
2. Show 2-3 sample videos in the exact format plus per-video views, average view duration/retention, and audience geography.
3. Offer a package (e.g., 3 Shorts + 1 carousel + usage rights window) and a pilot price; name the disclosure approach up front, since it reassures brand legal.
4. Channels: direct outreach to small/mid brands, creator marketplaces (e.g., Instagram Creator Marketplace, per https://www.contentgrip.com/meta-branded-content-rules-update/), and agencies.

### Media kit contents
Channel one-liner and niche; audience demographics and top countries; views per video (median, not best); retention/AVD; subscriber and follower counts per platform; format menu with deliverables; past results or case studies; brand-safety statement (topics avoided, AI-use statement, disclosure practice); rate card or "starting at" (only with your own data); contact and turnaround.

## 4. Rules and disclosure

### FTC [OFFICIAL]
- Disclose any "financial, employment, personal, or family relationship," including free or discounted products; if you got "anything of value," disclose (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).
- Placement: with the endorsement itself, not buried in profile or description; in video, inside the video, ideally both audio and visual; repeat periodically in livestreams. Use plain words ("ad," "sponsored"); avoid "sp," "spon," or bare "ambassador."
- Do not make unsupported claims or misrepresent untested products.
- 2023 revised Endorsement Guides: disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous" (difficult to miss, understandable) and unavoidable [SECONDARY summaries, e.g., https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2023/07/federal-trade-commission-releases-first-updates-to-endorsement-guides-in-14-years]. Secondary summaries say virtual/AI influencers are covered by the same standard; I did not confirm that wording in the guide text itself.
- Practical rule for us: voice-over says "sponsored by X" in the first seconds AND on-screen text AND the platform toggle. A synthetic narrator cannot "experience" a product; any first-person claim ("I use...") must be true of the channel's real operator or be rephrased as a demonstration. [OUR JUDGMENT]
- The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews/testimonials (including AI-generated) exists, per my background knowledge; not re-verified in this session. Check ftc.gov before relying on it.

### YouTube paid promotion [OFFICIAL]
- Tick "paid promotion" for sponsorships, endorsements, or other commercial relationships; YouTube adds a label at the start of the video (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235). Sponsored content must still meet Community Guidelines and ad policies. EU political paid promotion has a separate disclosure from 10 Oct 2025.
- The checkbox does not replace FTC-style on-screen/audio disclosure.

### YouTube altered/synthetic disclosure [SECONDARY news coverage of an official policy, https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/youtube-says-will-require-creators-label-ai-content-rcna143937]
- Required when content is realistic and could be mistaken for a real person, event or place: synthetic version of a real person's voice, face swaps, altered footage of real events/places.
- Not required for clearly unrealistic/animated content, special effects, or AI used for scripts, ideas, captions.
- Sensitive topics (health, news, elections, finance) get a prominent on-video label; others a description label.
- Implication: illustrated Remotion/manim visuals need no label; photoreal AI clips of real places or events in a news explainer do. A generic TTS voice that does not imitate a real person is not covered by the reported wording, but check the current help page before relying on this.

### Instagram / Meta
- Branded content tool: add "Paid partnership with [brand]" label to posts, Reels and Stories; "branded content ads" renamed "partnership ads" (https://www.contentgrip.com/meta-branded-content-rules-update/, [SECONDARY]).
- Per the same source: from March 2026 AI disclosure is required for commercial ads (brands manually disclose third-party AI creative tools), and Meta platform labels alone do not satisfy FTC; spoken disclosure within the first few seconds and caption disclosure before the "more" truncation is advised. Unverified against Meta primary docs.
- Instagram announced an "AI Creator" profile label on 4 May 2026 for accounts regularly producing AI content, toggled by the creator (https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/instagram-introduces-ai-creator-label-for-content-transparency-154400.html, [SECONDARY]). Whether to enable it is a decision to make; Meta also auto-labels content it detects as AI.

## 5. YouTube inauthentic / reused content policy (2026)

Official page (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) [OFFICIAL]:
- "Repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025. Monetized content should not be "mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative."
- Reused content: repurposing material from YouTube or elsewhere without "significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value." Applies at channel level; monetization can be removed across the channel.
- Not monetizable: AI-generated content from generic or unoriginal templates that gives the impression of mass production without the creator's original insight; AI-generated personas delivering information on sensitive topics (health, legal, finance).
- YouTube framed the July 2025 change as a "minor update" clarifying existing rules, not an AI ban (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/). Examples cited: narrative stories differing only superficially, slideshows with identical narration (https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/).
- I did not find an official 2026 amendment in this pass; re-check the page before launch.

Safe-operation checklist [OUR JUDGMENT, aligned with the above]:
1. Vary structure, hook type, visuals and pacing per video; do not ship one template with swapped nouns. Rotate 3-5 formats from Section 1.
2. Add original value in every script: a human-chosen angle, verified data, own calculation, own diagram.
3. Human reviews every script and fact; keep a source list per video.
4. Never reuse others' clips, music or voice without rights; AI clips only as illustration.
5. On finance, health, legal and news topics, avoid an AI persona presenting as an authority; use named sources, on-screen citations, and a real human accountable owner (voice or byline).
6. Throttle cadence to what a human can actually review; spikes of near-identical uploads are the pattern the policy describes.
7. Disclose realistic synthetic media; keep a record of tool and prompt per video.
8. Apply for YPP on organic traction; sponsorship income does not depend on YPP, so a monetization strike should not end the business.
