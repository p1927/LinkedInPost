# Psychology of Attention and Learning for 45-60s ELI5 Explainer Shorts

Researched 2026-10-05. Evidence labels: **[Strong]** = peer-reviewed meta-analysis or primary platform/regulator documentation; **[Moderate]** = single studies or reputable reviews; **[Folk]** = industry blog or practitioner lore, not verified; **[Unverified]** = could not confirm.

## 1. Attention and drop-off in short-form video

**Platform-stated signals (primary sources)**
- Instagram says Reels ranking uses four categories: your activity, your history with the creator, information about the reel (audio, visuals, popularity), and creator information. Its most important predictions are likelihood to reshare, watch all the way through, like, and visit the audio page. It also says it reduces reach for low-resolution or watermarked reels, muted reels, reels with borders, majority-text reels, and reposts of content already on Instagram. [S1][S2] **[Strong]**
- YouTube changed Shorts view counting on 2025-03-31: a view now counts when a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric was renamed "engaged views" and still drives Partner Program earnings. [S3] **[Moderate]** (reported via secondary coverage of the official announcement; the YouTube Help page I tried returned 404, so I could not read it directly).
- YouTube Studio shows a "viewed vs swiped away" split for Shorts. [S4] **[Moderate]** (secondary source).

**What I could NOT verify**
- A precise "hook window" (e.g. "3 seconds") and completion-rate tiers (e.g. "under 40% is poor, over 75% is excellent") appear only in marketing blogs [S4][S5]. No platform document or peer-reviewed study I found backs them. **[Folk / Unverified]** Treat them as hypotheses to test against our own retention graphs.
- Claims such as "sends carry 3-5x the weight of likes" come from secondary summaries of Mosseri comments [S5]. I did not locate the primary statement. **[Unverified]**

**Takeaway.** Platforms confirm that "watch through" and "reshare" are weighted signals. Everything numeric about hook seconds is folk wisdom. Design for the first moments anyway (cheap to do), but measure ourselves.

## 2. Learning science for explainers

**Mayer's multimedia principles.** A meta-analytic summary reports these effect sizes (Cohen's d): coherence 0.70, signaling 0.46, redundancy 0.87, spatial contiguity 0.79, temporal contiguity 1.30, segmenting 0.70, pre-training 0.46, modality 0.72. [S6] **[Strong]**
Applied to Shorts:
- **Coherence**: cut anything not serving the one idea (music with lyrics, decorative footage, tangents).
- **Signaling**: highlight the key word, arrow, or colour on the thing being named.
- **Redundancy**: narration plus identical on-screen full sentences hurts. Use short key-word captions, not a transcript duplicating narration. (Caveat: captions help comprehension for second-language listeners, g = .61 in one meta-analysis [S7]. Since many viewers watch muted, keep captions but make them short and keyword-led. This is my inference, not a tested rule for Shorts.)
- **Modality**: spoken narration with visuals beats on-screen text with visuals.
- **Segmenting**: one idea per ~10-15s beat. (The beat length is my design choice, not from the research.)
- **Temporal contiguity**: show the picture as the word is spoken.
- **Personalization** (conversational tone, "you"): listed by Mayer as a generative-processing principle [S6]; I did not retrieve its effect size. 

**Dual coding** (Paivio): verbal and visual information are processed in separate channels, so pairing them aids memory [S7] **[Moderate]**.

**Curiosity gap.** Loewenstein (1994) frames curiosity as arising when attention focuses on a gap in knowledge; some prior knowledge is required to feel the gap. [S8] **[Strong theory, applied evidence for video retention is unverified]**. Practical reading: open with a question the viewer can almost answer, not one about something totally unfamiliar.

**Spacing and retrieval.** Dunlosky et al. (2013) rate distributed practice and practice testing as high-utility techniques. [S9] **[Strong]** For Shorts this means: a one-line recap at the end, and a series that revisits earlier concepts across episodes. Whether a 5-second recap within a single Short lifts memory is **[Unverified]**.

**Open loops / prediction**: I found no rigorous study on "open loops" in short video. Prediction-before-reveal is plausible from curiosity theory but **[Folk]** in this format.

## 3. Emotion, surprise, humour

- **Seductive details** (interesting but irrelevant additions) reduce learning: a meta-analysis of 58 studies found a small-to-moderate negative effect; effect was larger for static images and when placed at the end. A newer 2026 meta-analysis (50 studies) points to extraneous cognitive load as the mechanism. [S10] **[Strong]**
- **Emotional design** (warm colours, friendly character design integrated with the lesson) shows small positive effects: retention 0.35, transfer 0.27, comprehension 0.29, with the caveat that effects vanish or reverse when the emotional element does not align with the learning goal. [S11] **[Moderate to Strong]**
- **Implication**: humour and surprise should *be* the explanation (a funny analogy that carries the mechanism), not a side gag. Jokes about unrelated topics are seductive details.
- Emotional arcs, surprise as a memory enhancer, and humour effects in explainers: I did not retrieve direct evidence for short-form video. **[Unverified]**

## 4. Explaining to a child

- Analogies let learners connect new ideas to existing knowledge, but misconceptions arise when parts of the model are misleading, missing, or misapplied. [S12][S13] **[Moderate]**
- Children use analogies spontaneously, and those can lead to misconceptions. [S13] **[Moderate]**
- **Not found**: robust evidence on optimal vocabulary level or sentence length for child-directed explainer video. Common "short sentences, simple words" advice is plausible but **[Unverified]**. Do not cite numeric targets (e.g. "grade 3 reading level") as research-backed.
- **Practical safeguard** (our inference): for each analogy, state where it breaks ("the bank is like a...but unlike a piggy bank, it lends your money out"), and avoid analogies whose wrong parts are more memorable than the right ones.

## 5. Sponsored content psychology

- **Regulatory baseline (FTC)**: disclosures must sit alongside the endorsement, not only in a profile or "more" section or at the end of a video; use plain words ("ad", "sponsored", "thanks to Brand X for the free product"), avoid vague tags like "sp" or "collab"; in video, disclose in both audio and visual form within the video itself; do not rely on the platform's tool alone. [S14] **[Strong]**
- **Disclosure effects**: a meta-analysis of 61 papers found disclosure reduces brand attitude, credibility and source evaluation, while raising recognition, persuasion knowledge and resistance; effects depend on disclosure wording, timing and awareness. [S15] **[Strong]**
- A more recent meta-analysis reports a "disclosure paradox": recognition rises without consistently harming brand evaluations, and standardized platform labels and prominent in-video overlays beat creator-entered captions for recognition. [S16] **[Moderate]** (single-journal source; I did not independently check its quality).
- **Native vs interruptive**: I did not retrieve a primary study comparing native and interruptive ads for explainer content. The idea that integrated ads annoy less is **[Folk]**.
- **Ethical lines (our recommendation, aligned with FTC)**: disclose early and clearly; the sponsor example must be factually accurate and actually illustrate the concept; no health or financial claims we cannot support; if the sponsor does not fit the concept, do not force it. Disclosure costs some brand warmth but protects trust and compliance.

## 6. Rules checklist mapped to our pipeline

| Stage | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Open with a concrete question or surprising fact the viewer partly knows (creates a gap). Visual and spoken in the first moments. Test hook length ourselves. | Loewenstein [S8] strong; timing folk |
| **Script** | One idea, one analogy, 3-5 beats. Cut anything not serving the idea. Each beat segments a step. | Coherence, segmenting [S6] strong |
| **Script** | Concrete example before abstract term; name the term after the picture. | Analogy research [S12] moderate |
| **Script** | State where the analogy breaks, in one line. | Misconception risk [S13] moderate |
| **Script** | Humour only if it carries the mechanism. | Seductive details [S10] strong |
| **Voice** | Conversational "you" tone; human-sounding narration, spoken not text. Pace: **no evidence found for a specific words-per-minute**, so A/B test. | Personalization, modality [S6]; pace unverified |
| **Visuals** | Picture appears as the word is spoken; highlight the key object; no decorative clutter; no watermark, borders, or majority-text frames; 1080p+. | Contiguity, signaling [S6]; Instagram [S1] |
| **Captions** | Short keyword captions, not a duplicate transcript; keep for muted viewers. | Redundancy [S6]; captions [S7] |
| **Music** | Low, instrumental, no lyrics, ducked under voice. | Coherence [S6] (inference for music) |
| **Recap** | One-line closing recap; callbacks to earlier episodes in a series. | Spacing/retrieval [S9] strong; in-Short effect unverified |
| **Sponsor** | Disclose in audio and on-screen early, plain words ("ad", "sponsored"). Sponsor story must be accurate and relevant to the concept. | FTC [S14], meta-analysis [S15] |
| **CTA** | Ask for a share ("send to someone who...") since reshare is a named ranking prediction; keep it single and short. | Instagram [S2]; CTA wording untested |
| **Measure** | Track viewed vs swiped away and average view duration; treat blog benchmarks as hypotheses. | YouTube [S3][S4] |

## Gaps to close with our own data
Hook timing, narration pace, recap length, native-vs-interruptive sponsor placement, child-appropriate vocabulary: none have solid published support for 45-60s vertical video. Run A/B tests.
