# Psychology Rules Checklist

Mapped to pipeline stages. Tags: [E] sourced evidence (peer-reviewed or primary regulator), [V] vendor/blog claim, [F] folk wisdom/craft heuristic.
Full source details in docs/research/video/01-psychology-of-attention-and-learning.md.

---

## Hook

- [ ] Open with a concrete question or surprising fact the viewer can almost—but not quite—answer. Prior knowledge is required to feel a knowledge gap. [E, Loewenstein 1994 curiosity theory]
- [ ] The hook must be visible in frame AND spoken in narration within the first few seconds. Test hook timing against your own retention graphs; no published numeric threshold is reliable. [E for platform watch-through weighting; [F] for specific second counts]
- [ ] Do not open with a concept totally unfamiliar to the viewer—curiosity requires a gap in existing knowledge, not a void. [E, Loewenstein]

---

## Script

- [ ] One idea per video; cut anything that does not serve it. [E, Mayer coherence principle, d=0.70]
- [ ] One beat per ~10–15 s segment; each beat advances one step. Beat length is a design choice, not a research finding. [E principle; [F] timing]
- [ ] Concrete example shown and named BEFORE the true technical term is introduced. [E, analogy research, moderate; [F] timing]
- [ ] State where the analogy breaks—one line in the script. [E, misconception risk, moderate]
- [ ] Humour must carry the mechanism; unrelated jokes are seductive details that reduce learning. [E, seductive-details meta-analysis, 58 studies, strong]
- [ ] Emotional elements (warm character, funny scenario) must align with the learning goal or their benefit vanishes. [E, emotional design meta-analysis, moderate-strong: retention d=0.35, transfer d=0.27]
- [ ] Cap open loops at 2 at once; every loop must have a logged payoff beat. [F]
- [ ] Every factual claim gets a source URL; no invented statistics. [E, FTC for sponsor claims; [F] for accuracy generally]

---

## Voice

- [ ] Use conversational "you" and "your" throughout. [E, Mayer personalization principle]
- [ ] Spoken narration with visuals beats on-screen text with visuals. [E, Mayer modality principle, d=0.72]
- [ ] Budget words at 2.5 words/second (150 wpm) for planning; run TTS and adjust—no rigorously proven pace for Shorts. [V, voice-over industry; pace effectiveness unverified]
- [ ] Sentences average 12 words; maximum 18; vary length for rhythm. [F]
- [ ] Read the script aloud before approval; fix tongue twisters. [F]

---

## Visuals

- [ ] Show the picture as the word is spoken (temporal contiguity). [E, Mayer d=1.30, strongest effect]
- [ ] Highlight the key object or word on screen at the moment it is named (signaling). [E, d=0.46]
- [ ] Cut decorative footage not serving the idea (coherence). [E, d=0.70]
- [ ] No watermarks, borders, or majority-text frames—platforms penalize them in ranking. [E, Instagram ranking documentation]
- [ ] Minimum resolution 1080 × 1920. [E, Instagram]
- [ ] Visual change at least every ~4 s; a static hold is intentional when showing something the viewer must read. [R, no rigorous data on optimal cut rate]

---

## Captions

- [ ] Short keyword captions in word-group ("karaoke") style—NOT a full transcript duplicating narration. Full-sentence duplicates hurt learning via the redundancy effect. [E, Mayer redundancy principle, d=0.87]
- [ ] Keep captions for muted viewers; short captions also help second-language viewers. [E, captioning meta-analysis, g=0.61 for comprehension; inference applied to Shorts]
- [ ] Caption contrast ≥ 4.5:1 on the worst background frame. [E, WCAG 2.x]

---

## Music

- [ ] Instrumental only—no lyrics under narration. [E, Mayer coherence principle, applied to music; inference]
- [ ] Keep music 10–15 dB below speech. Duck further during key lines. [E secondary, mixing guides]
- [ ] 0.3–0.6 s drop of music before a key reveal adds emphasis. [R]

---

## CTA

- [ ] One CTA only, after the payoff. [F]
- [ ] Frame as a reshare ("send this to someone who…") since reshare is a named Instagram ranking signal. [E, Instagram ranking documentation; CTA wording effects untested]
- [ ] One-line recap or callback in the closing beat supports memory. A single in-Short recap may not lift retention on its own; series revisiting earlier concepts does (distributed practice). [E, Dunlosky 2013 spacing/retrieval strong; in-Short recap effect unverified]

---

## Sponsor

- [ ] Disclose in narration AND on screen; use plain words ("ad", "sponsored by X"). [E, FTC Endorsement Guides]
- [ ] Disclosure reduces brand attitude slightly but raises recognition; prominent in-video overlays beat creator-entered captions for recognition. [E, meta-analysis of 61 papers, strong; disclosure paradox meta-analysis, moderate]
- [ ] Sponsor story must be factually accurate and genuinely illustrate the concept being explained. No health or financial claims without substantiation. [E, FTC; [F] editorial rule]
- [ ] A synthetic narrator cannot experientially endorse a product; rephrase first-person claims as demonstrations. [OUR JUDGMENT, aligned with FTC AI coverage intent]
